Health Insurance in Slovakia: The Expat's Complete Guide

Who must enroll, which insurer to pick, what it costs, and what happens if you miss the 8-day deadline. Verified against official Slovak legislation.

The short version

Health insurance in Slovakia is mandatory for everyone who works or lives here legally. The system is governed by Act No. 580/2004 Coll. on Health Insurance. Unlike many countries, Slovakia does not have a single national insurer, you choose from three competing zdravotné poisťovne (health insurance funds). Your employer pays most of your premium; self-employed people and voluntary insurees pay the full minimum themselves.

⏱️

The 8-day rule. Under § 6(1) of Act 580/2004, you must register with an insurer within 8 days of the day your insurance obligation begins (first day of employment, start of self-employment, or acquisition of permanent residence). Missing this window results in a fine and a gap in coverage, see Deadlines & penalties below.

Who must have Slovak health insurance

Slovak law covers a wide group. You fall under the public system if you are:

  • Employed in Slovakia (even part-time) by a Slovak employer, your employer registers you and co-pays
  • Self-employed (SZČO / živnostník) operating in Slovakia
  • A holder of Slovak permanent residence, you must join even if you're not working
  • A dependent child or student who is considered a poistenec štátu (state-insured person, the state pays for them)

You are not obliged to join the public system if you are:

  • A tourist or short-term visitor (under 90 days)
  • An EU citizen with valid EHIC or comprehensive travel insurance, for temporary stays only
  • A student on a Slovak university visa who is covered by a parent's EU insurer
  • Waiting for your residence permit with valid commercial insurance that meets Slovak visa requirements
ℹ️

If you're working remotely for a foreign employer from Slovakia, the situation is more complex, Slovak social security rules may still apply depending on your tax residency. Consult a Slovak accountant or the Social Insurance Agency to clarify your specific case.

The three public insurers

You have exactly three options. All are licensed by the Úrad pre dohľad nad zdravotnou starostlivosťou (ÚDZS), the Healthcare Surveillance Authority, and all provide the same legally-mandated basic benefits package. Competition exists in the extras: wellness programmes, dental contribution top-ups, discounts at gyms and pharmacies.

Union is the most expat-friendly. It is currently the only Slovak public insurer with a fully English online registration process and English customer service. If you don't speak Slovak yet, start with Union. You can switch insurer once per year (from 1 January, with application by 30 September of the prior year).

How to register, step by step

If you are employed

Your Slovak employer is legally responsible for registering you. They will ask which insurer you choose, then notify that insurer and start deducting contributions from your salary. Your job: tell HR which of the three insurers you want before your first day (or at the latest within 8 days). If you say nothing, VšZP is typically used as the default.

1

Choose your insurer

Compare the three options above. For English support, choose Union. For the largest network and most client-friendly extras in 2025–2026, check current reviews, the balance shifts yearly.

2

Tell your HR department

Hand them the insurer's name and your national ID or passport. They submit a registration form (Prihlásenie zamestnávateľa / zamestnanca) and you receive a preukaz poistenca (insurance card) within a few weeks by post or email.

3

Activate your online account

Each insurer has a patient portal. Activate it to book appointments, request referrals, and download confirmations of insurance (needed for many bureaucratic purposes in Slovakia).

If you are self-employed (živnostník / SZČO)

You must register yourself directly with your chosen insurer. The deadline is 8 days from the date you started your business activity (typically the date your trade license takes effect).

📋 What to bring / submit (SZČO registration)
  • Completed registration form, available at each insurer's branch or website
  • Valid passport or Slovak ID/residence card
  • Your trade license (živnostenský list) or confirmation of registration at the Trade Office
  • Slovak address (required for correspondence)

You can register in person at any branch of your chosen insurer or, for Union, entirely online. Once registered, you will receive your insurance number and payment instructions for monthly contributions.

If you have permanent residence but are not working

You are a dobrovoľne poistená osoba (voluntarily insured person) and pay the full minimum contribution yourself. Register directly with your chosen insurer the same way as self-employed, but use the "voluntary" category on the form.

How much it costs

Contributions are a percentage of your income, with a legally-set minimum. Rates are set in Act 580/2004 and updated periodically. The 2025 figures confirmed by ÚDZS are:

What is covered

All three insurers are required by law to cover the same basic package, set by the Ministry of Health. For expats, this means:

  • GP visits, you register with a general practitioner (obvodný lekár); referrals needed for specialists
  • Specialist consultations with a referral from your GP or insurer
  • Emergency care, always covered regardless of insurer; no referral needed
  • Hospitalisation, covered; co-pay of €1.99/day for inpatient stays
  • Prescription medicines, partially reimbursed; you pay a co-pay at the pharmacy
  • Maternity care and delivery
  • Preventive screenings, annual check-up, colorectal cancer screening, mammography, etc.

Not covered: most dental work (only emergency extractions), cosmetic procedures, most eyeglasses and contact lenses, private clinic surcharges. Each insurer offers supplementary contribution programmes for these, check your insurer's current doplnkové programy.

EHIC and private/commercial insurance

European Health Insurance Card (EHIC)

If you're an EU/EEA citizen, your EHIC covers medically necessary care during a temporary stay in Slovakia, at the same conditions as Slovak insured persons. However, EHIC is a temporary travel document, not a substitute for Slovak public insurance once you are a legal resident or employee. As soon as you start working or obtain permanent residence, you must join the Slovak public system. Your EHIC from your home country becomes irrelevant from that point.

Private/commercial health insurance

If you are not yet obliged to join the public system (e.g. you are waiting for your residence permit, or you are a short-term visitor), you must have private commercial insurance that meets Slovak visa requirements:

  • Coverage of at least €30,000 for emergency medical care and repatriation
  • Valid throughout the Schengen Area
  • No excess/deductible higher than what the Slovak consulate accepts (varies by consulate)

Recognised providers used by expats in Slovakia include AXA, Allianz, UNIQA, and international expat plans (Cigna, Aetna, Bupa). Once you are legally required to be in the public system, private insurance is not a legal substitute, you must enroll in one of the three public funds.

⚠️

Late registration penalty. Under Act 580/2004 § 24, ÚDZS can fine you up to €3,319 for late registration and up to €9,958 for repeated violations. Additionally, any healthcare received during the uninsured gap can be billed to you directly at full price. Don't delay.

FAQ

Can I use my foreign insurer in Slovakia?

Only temporarily. If you become a legal employee or permanent resident, you must join one of the three Slovak public insurers. Your foreign insurer does not satisfy Slovak law.

What if I get sick before my insurance card arrives?

Registration creates the coverage obligation from day one, the physical card is just proof. Ask your insurer for a written confirmation of registration (potvrdenie o poistení), which doctors and hospitals accept immediately.

Do I pay health insurance even when unemployed?

If you are registered as a job seeker at the Labour Office (úrad práce), the state covers your contributions. If you are not registered and have no income, you are a voluntary insuree and must pay the minimum contribution (€107.25/month in 2025) yourself.

Can I switch insurer?

Yes, once per calendar year. Submit a switch request to your new insurer by 30 September; the change takes effect on 1 January of the following year.

Is dental covered?

Only emergency dental extractions are covered as a basic benefit. Fillings, crowns, orthodontics, and implants are out-of-pocket, though each insurer offers some supplementary contribution. Budget €50–200 for basic dental work; dental clinics in Slovakia are significantly cheaper than Western Europe.
