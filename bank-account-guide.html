Opening a Bank Account in Slovakia as a Foreigner

Which banks actually accept non-residents, what documents you need, and the fastest path to a working Slovak IBAN. Practical, honest, no fluff.

The short version

Slovak banks are regulated by the National Bank of Slovakia (NBS) and required by EU law to offer a basic payment account to any EU resident. In practice, most of the big banks want to see a Slovak address and some connection to Slovakia, an employment contract, trade license, or at least a residence registration certificate. Without these, you'll face more friction.

The fastest path to a Slovak IBAN while you're still getting settled is a neo-bank (Revolut, Wise, N26), open one from your home country before you arrive, use it immediately, and switch to a local bank once you have your Slovak documents in order.

💡

Pro tip. Your Slovak employer's payroll team will ask for a Slovak bank account number for salary payments. Open at least a Revolut account before your first payday, Slovak payroll systems accept IBAN from any EU bank, not just Slovak ones.

Do you actually need a Slovak bank account?

For many expats, especially short-term, the answer is no, a Revolut or Wise account with a European IBAN handles 90% of daily needs (salary, rent, utilities, online shopping). A Slovak bank account becomes useful, or necessary, when:

  • Your employer specifically requires a Slovak IBAN (rare but happens)
  • You need a Slovak address-linked account for a mortgage or loan
  • You want to pay your živnosť social/health insurance contributions via SEPA standing order without foreign transaction fees
  • You have Slovak clients who prefer local bank transfers
  • You accumulate large amounts in EUR and want Slovak deposit guarantee protection (up to €100,000 per bank)

Slovenská sporiteľňa (SLSP)

The largest bank in Slovakia by client count. Part of the Erste Group. Accepts non-residents but expects you to demonstrate a connection to Slovakia, typically a work contract, trade license, or proof of property ownership.

📋 SLSP, documents typically required
  • Valid passport or EU national ID card
  • Proof of connection to Slovakia: employment contract, trade license, or residence registration certificate (potvrdenie o pobyte)
  • Slovak address (for correspondence, can be a friend's address initially if disclosed)
  • Tax identification number if self-employed

You can start the opening process via the SLSP app (George), but in-person verification at a branch is required for non-residents. English-speaking staff are available at most Bratislava city-centre branches.

Tatra banka

Part of Raiffeisen Group, widely considered the most expat-friendly traditional Slovak bank. The app (Tatra banka) and internet banking are well-designed and partially available in English. For non-Slovak residents, Tatra banka's standard requirement is an employment contract with a Slovak employer or confirmation of self-employment. Some branches will open an account for EU citizens on just a passport + Slovak address.

💡

Best bet for employed expats. If your employer has a corporate relationship with Tatra banka (common for multinational companies in Bratislava), opening a personal account as a new employee is usually fast-tracked, ask your HR department before going to a branch on your own.

VÚB banka

Part of Intesa Sanpaolo Group. VÚB has historically been the most restrictive for non-residents: their cross-border opening procedure (for EU citizens without Slovak residence) requires a paper application form in Slovak with an authenticated signature and submission in person. This procedure exists but is not widely advertised. In practice, if you already have a Slovak residence registration certificate and a Slovak address, opening at a branch is straightforward. If you do not, expect to be redirected to wait until you have that documentation.

mBank & Fio banka

mBank (Polish-owned, operates in Slovakia, Czechia, Poland) is one of the more flexible options for EU citizens. Online application possible; the process is in Slovak but manageable with a browser translator. Monthly fee is typically the lowest of the major banks (often €0 with a minimum monthly transaction).

Fio banka (Czech-Slovak bank) offers a basic free current account that EU citizens can open relatively easily. Their English support is limited, but the application process is short and can be partially completed online before visiting a branch.

Neo-banks: Revolut, Wise, N26

For most expats arriving in Slovakia, a neo-bank account is the immediate practical solution while traditional bank paperwork gets sorted. Key points:

  • Revolut, Lithuanian IBAN (LT...), fully EUR-capable, instant top-up, widely accepted in Slovakia. Open from anywhere with a selfie and ID. Free tier is sufficient for most needs.
  • Wise, Belgian IBAN (BE...) or multi-currency. Excellent for receiving salary in EUR and converting to other currencies. Very transparent fees.
  • N26, German IBAN (DE...). Full current account. Requires EU residence for signup; if you haven't registered your Slovak address yet, use your home country address initially.
⚠️

Limitations of neo-banks in Slovakia. Slovak landlords and some utilities providers still prefer Slovak IBAN (SK...) for direct debit mandates. A few older Slovak companies' payment systems reject non-Slovak IBANs despite EU rules requiring acceptance. Having a traditional Slovak account as a backup is worth it once you're settled.

Documents you will need

Regardless of which bank you choose, have these ready:

📋 Standard document package
  • Valid passport or EU national ID card (non-EU nationals: passport only)
  • Proof of Slovak address: rental contract, utility bill in your name, or residence registration certificate from the Foreign Police
  • Proof of income or connection to Slovakia: employment contract, trade license confirmation, or letter from employer
  • Slovak tax ID number (DIČ) if self-employed, register at your local tax office or via slovensko.sk before opening the account
  • Second ID document (helpful but not always required): driver's license, old passport
ℹ️

Non-EU nationals face additional scrutiny due to AML/KYC requirements. Your bank will ask about the source of funds and may require additional documentation (home country bank statement, proof of employment). Processing times can be 2–4 weeks rather than the same-day opening EU citizens experience.

FAQ

Can I open an account before I arrive in Slovakia?

For traditional Slovak banks, no, in-person verification is required. For Revolut, Wise, or N26, yes, you can open remotely from your home country using your passport. This is the recommended approach: arrive with a working European IBAN already set up.

Do I need a Slovak address to open an account?

Most traditional banks require some Slovak address for correspondence. In practice, a rental contract or a residence registration certificate (even a short-term one) is usually sufficient. VÚB is the strictest; SLSP and Tatra banka have more flexibility in practice.

Will my salary be paid in EUR?

Yes, Slovakia uses the Euro (EUR). All local salaries are in EUR. Currency conversion is not an issue within Slovakia.

What if my account application is refused?

Under EU Payment Accounts Directive (2014/92/EU), any EU resident is entitled to a basic payment account at a bank operating in Slovakia, refusal must be justified. If refused, ask the bank for the written reason. For complaints, contact the National Bank of Slovakia (financial market supervisor) or the Finančný arbiter (Financial Arbitrator) for dispute resolution.
